Chapter 5 (c) IQR and outlier fences

q1 <- quantile(airquality\(Temp, 0.25) q3 <- quantile(airquality\)Temp, 0.75) iqr <- IQR(airquality$Temp) cat(“Lower fence:”, q1 - 1.5iqr, ” Upper fence:“, q3 + 1.5iqr, “”)

✅ Answer

(a) summary() returns: Min = 56, Q1 = 72, Median = 79, Q3 = 85, Max = 97.

(b) mean(airquality$Temp) ≈ 77.9°F; sd(airquality$Temp) ≈ 9.47°F. The mean (77.9) is very close to the median (79), suggesting the distribution of temperatures is approximately symmetric (slightly left-skewed).

(c) IQR = Q3 − Q1 = 85 − 72 = 13. Lower fence = 72 − 19.5 = 52.5; upper fence = 85 + 19.5 = 104.5. All temperatures are in the range 56–97, so no outliers.

R Exercise 2

Still using airquality, the variable Wind records the average wind speed in miles per hour (mph).

  1. Compute the mean, median, variance, and standard deviation of Wind.
  2. Is the mean greater than or less than the median? What does this suggest about the shape of the distribution?
  3. Modify the code to also display summary(airquality$Wind). Does this confirm your answer to (b)?
Interactive R — Wind Speed Statistics

✅ Answer

(a) mean(wind) ≈ 9.96 mph; median(wind) = 9.70 mph; var(wind) ≈ 12.41; sd(wind) ≈ 3.52 mph.

(b) The mean (9.96) is slightly greater than the median (9.70). This suggests the distribution is mildly right-skewed — a few high wind-speed days pull the mean upward.

(c) summary(wind) gives: Min = 1.7, Q1 = 7.4, Median = 9.7, Q3 = 11.5, Max = 20.7. The max of 20.7 is noticeably higher than Q3 (11.5), consistent with a slight right skew.

R Exercise 3

Create a histogram of airquality$Temp using base R’s hist() function.

  1. Run the starter code to produce the histogram. Describe the shape of the distribution.
  2. Modify breaks = 10 to breaks = 5. How does the choice of bin count affect what you can see in the histogram?
  3. Add the line abline(v = mean(airquality$Temp), col = "red", lwd = 2) after hist(...). What does this line represent?
Interactive R — Histogram of Temperature

✅ Answer

(a) The histogram shows a roughly symmetric distribution, centred around 75–80°F, with slightly more observations in the upper half. There is a mild left-skew — the left tail extends further than the right.

(b) With breaks = 5, each bar covers a wider range, hiding finer detail (e.g., the small dip around 60–65°F disappears). More breaks reveal more structure; fewer breaks show a smoother, simpler shape. The choice involves a bias–variance trade-off.

(c) The red vertical line abline(v = mean(...)) marks the sample mean (≈ 77.9°F). Visually, it should fall near the centre of the distribution, which is consistent with the roughly symmetric shape observed.

R Exercise 4

Create a boxplot of airquality$Temp grouped by Month (month coded as integers 5 = May, 6 = June, …, 9 = September).

  1. Run the starter code. Which month has the highest median temperature? Which has the most variability (widest box)?
  2. Does any month show outliers (dots beyond the whiskers)?
  3. Modify col to use a different colour. What other arguments can you pass to boxplot()? Try adding notch = TRUE — what does the notch represent?
Interactive R — Temperature by Month (Boxplot)

✅ Answer

(a) July (Month 7) has the highest median temperature (approximately 81°F). May (Month 5) has the widest box (greatest IQR), indicating the most spread in daily temperatures that month.

(b) May shows a low outlier — one day with an unusually cold temperature (56°F). No other months show clear outlier dots.

(c) The notch in a boxplot displays an approximate 95% confidence interval for the population median. If two notches do not overlap, there is strong evidence that the two group medians differ. For example, July’s notch does not overlap with May’s, confirming that July is significantly warmer on average.
